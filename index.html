Am I a designer?

Notes on how I see design, through the necklaces I make.

Filippo Maria Santilli

01

Opening

I do not think design belongs only to designers. I think we all design our lives, each in our own way, around what makes us happier. We think about something, and then we build it, for ourselves or for the people close to us.

So when I ask myself if I am a designer, I do not start from a method or from a product. I start from a simpler question: what have I really designed, with care, and what does it say about the way I think? The answer that keeps coming back is a handful of necklaces.

I want to be clear about why I am showing them. They are not the subject of this essay. They are the biggest piece of design in my life so far, and the clearest place where I can show you how I see it. This page is a small exhibition of them. Through them, I would like you to enter a bit into my world.

02

Designing a life

This is me at four or five, in my first year of playing football. I played until I was 20.

I was a player who liked to create. I made a lot of assists, I had good technique, and I liked what was beautiful to watch on the pitch, not only what worked. I did not call that design back then, but I was already looking for the version of a play that was beautiful as well as effective.

Around 20 I reached a point where I had to choose: start university, or keep playing at a high level. I chose university. Football no longer gave me the calm it used to give me, and it was a world with rules I did not share, and still don't. The break was sudden, but it felt natural. Something had changed in me, in what I liked, and I followed it.

If I take my own idea seriously, that we design our lives around what makes us happier, this was the first big piece of design I did, long before any object. I did not make anything. I decided what I wanted around me, and what I did not.

03

What I look at

When football stopped taking all of my attention, I began to look at what was around me. Fashion came first. I like to understand the idea behind a brand. I like to build outfits that say who I am, how I think, what I like to do. For me fashion is the simplest and the nicest way to express who you are. Clothes and accessories are one of the first things people see in you, so they tell something about you before you speak.

I am not an expert in any of this. I go to exhibitions and museums, but I am not a connoisseur. I do not know the technique of photography. What I know is what I like: beauty, but only when there is a thought behind it. One of my favorite clothing brands says "art without meaning is just aesthetics." I do not like beauty with nothing behind it. That is the closest thing I have to a design principle.

04

Necklaces

I started making necklaces about a year and a half ago, in Lisbon, with my girlfriend. We went around looking at materials, trying to understand how to build one. I waited until I was back in Rome to buy everything, in the bead shops you see in these photos. One afternoon at my place we made the first one, for me. Neither of us knew how, so we learned together, and that is how it started. I still do it.

It did not stay a thing for me for long. I always imagined I would make them more for other people than for myself, and more for the pleasure of it than to sell them. I have always preferred doing things for others, and I have always thought of a gift as something felt and personal, especially when I made it myself.

At the shop. Strands, stones and pendants, sorted by color and material.

Everything I buy, every bead and every shell, I buy because I find it beautiful. So in a way I only put together things I already like. But the design, for me, is not in the beads. It is in the question of who the necklace is for, because that is where the thought behind it comes from.

05

Making one for someone

For my friend Riccardo's birthday I did not want a gift made just to give something. I wanted something personal, that could say who he is. Riccardo surfs and he loves that lifestyle, so I used a lot of colored stones and built a necklace with a surfer feeling. It was the first one I made for someone else. I was a beginner, and it came out very well. It is one of the best I have made, and he still wears it often.

For my friend Lisa it was different. She had told me several times that she liked my necklaces and that she wanted a choker, tighter at the neck, feminine, with a lot of color. She likes smileys, so they went in. It was one of the last days of summer. The next morning I made it, without telling her, and a few days later I gave it to her. She did not expect it, and I liked seeing her surprised and happy. I chose the colors thinking about her, her face, the way she dresses. I wanted them to make her shine.

In both cases I was doing the same thing: taking what I know about a person, what they like, how they look, how they live, and translating it into stones and colors.

06

Getting it wrong

Not all of them worked. The first necklace I made for my girlfriend is not good, looking at it now. It looks more like something for a lady than for a girl. I would never make it like that today. I take it as a sign that I learned something along the way.

Another one, for a friend last year, broke several times. I repaired it again and again. In the end I made him a different one for his birthday, and that is the one he wears now. You can see him in the photos.

Three sold on Vinted.

I also sold some necklaces on Vinted. Paradoxically, these are the ones I like the least. I think it is because, when I make one for a friend, I try harder without noticing. With a stranger there is no one to think about, so the thought behind it is missing. I would rather make someone I know happy than make money from this, at least for now. If I ever turn it into a business, of course, I will have to charge for my work.

To me these stories say the same thing. A necklace is only as good as my understanding of the person who will wear it, and I learn more about that every time I get one wrong.

07

Tied

A necklace ends with a knot, the point where it closes. And each one is tied to a person, the one it was made for. That is why the name is Tied.

Tied is not a business. I never started it as a project, mostly because I was afraid of being judged, and because opening a brand is hard, both financially and logistically. This assignment may be the occasion to start taking it more seriously. So this page is the first time I show it.

Tied. Necklaces, and the people wearing them.

08

So, am I a designer?

If design is thinking about something and then building it, for ourselves or for the people close to us, then yes, I am a designer, like everyone. Maybe a bit more than others, only because my passions are closer to what most people call design: fashion, making accessories, photography, art. I am not technical in any of them. I just like beauty with a reason behind it.

How I do it, I can say precisely. I start from a person and from things I find beautiful, and I look for the reason to put them together. When, I cannot give a date. I can only point at things: a boy in a goal, a choice at 20, an afternoon at home with a bag of beads. I noticed only afterwards that they had something in common.

Today I think I know what it is. Making necklaces gives me freedom, the kind football stopped giving me. I am free to think, to be creative, to do things my way. And I can always put people first, not the result. In football it was often the opposite: the score came before the fun, and before what made people happy.

The first time I saw someone wearing one of my necklaces, I felt something I still feel every time it happens. It is happiness, in the simplest sense of the word. The happiness of making someone happy with something I made myself. I think it is the most beautiful part of all of this for me.

If one day I stopped, I would miss making something that is mine. I would miss my small way of being part of fashion. But most of all I would miss that moment: a friend receiving a necklace with joy, and then seeing it on them, day after day.

So yes, I think I am a designer. I make small things for the people I care about, and that is the part of design I would never want to give up.
